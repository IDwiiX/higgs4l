\noindent
This note analyses the decay $H\to ZZ^*\to 4\ell$ with the ATLAS Open Data for education
(13\,TeV, 2015--2016, 36.6\,fb$^{-1}$)~\cite{opendata}. Starting from the official ATLAS
notebook~\cite{notebook}, it measures the Higgs boson mass, computes the significance of the
signal with a profile likelihood, and tests whether a simple classifier improves the result.

\section{Data and selection}

Events with exactly four leptons are compared with simulation of the Higgs signal
($m_H = 125\GeV$), the $ZZ^*$ background and smaller backgrounds. Each simulated event is
weighted by luminosity, cross section and detector scale factors. The selection first
reproduces the official notebook exactly, then pairs the leptons into two $Z$ candidates
(same flavour, opposite charge), with $50 < m_{12} < 106\GeV$ and $m_{34} > 12\GeV$.
Figure~\ref{fig:m4l} shows the four-lepton mass: the Higgs boson appears as a narrow peak
near 125\GeV\ above the $ZZ^*$ background.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{m4l_paired.pdf}
\caption{Four-lepton invariant mass: data and stacked simulation.}
\label{fig:m4l}
\end{figure}

\section{Higgs boson mass}

The 84 data events with $110<\mfl<140\GeV$ are fitted with an unbinned maximum-likelihood
fit: a Crystal Ball peak for the signal, with its shape taken from simulation, on top of an
exponential background. The method was first tested on 2000 simulated pseudo-experiments,
which showed an unbiased result with correctly estimated uncertainties. The fit to data gives
\begin{equation}
m_H = 125.06^{+0.93}_{-0.92}\GeV \quad \text{(statistical uncertainty)},
\end{equation}
in agreement with the ATLAS measurement in this channel, $124.79\pm0.37\GeV$~\cite{atlas2018mass},
and with the world average, $125.13\pm0.11\GeV$~\cite{pdg}.

\section{Significance}

The four-lepton mass spectrum between 110 and 160\GeV\ is fitted with pyhf~\cite{pyhf}, with
the signal strength $\mu$ as the parameter of interest and a free background normalization.
The observed significance is $3.96\sigma$, for $6.64\sigma$ expected, and the fitted signal
strength is $\hat\mu = 0.72^{+0.22}_{-0.20}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:fits}, right).
Table~\ref{tab:z} shows how simple estimates compare with the full likelihood fit.

\section{Classifier}

A logistic-regression classifier was trained on ten lepton kinematic variables, excluding the
four-lepton mass, to separate signal from background. It was trained on half of the
simulation and tested on the other half, reaching an area under the ROC curve of 0.70.
Splitting the events into two categories by classifier score did not improve the expected
significance (5.97 against 6.06 without categories).

\section{Conclusion}

With 36.6\,fb$^{-1}$ of open ATLAS data, the four-lepton channel gives
$m_H = 125.06^{+0.93}_{-0.92}\GeV$ and an observed signal significance of $3.96\sigma$. The
comparison of methods shows that a full likelihood fit gives a lower, more realistic
significance than simple formulas.
